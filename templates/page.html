Research

My research investigates questions concerning reality, meaning, knowledge, human existence, and responsibility through a Qur'an-centered engagement with hermeneutics, metaphysics, philosophy of science, and related interdisciplinary fields.

Research Areas

1. Qur'anic Studies & Hermeneutics

Research examining the Qur'an through Qur'an-centered interpretation, semantic analysis, textual structure, and engagement with both classical and contemporary approaches to Qur'anic meaning.

2. Metaphysics & Ontology

Research concerning the nature of reality, existence and becoming, intelligibility, causation, meaning, ontological structure, and the relationship between metaphysical questions and Qur'anic thought.

3. Philosophy of Science

Research concerning scientific explanation, methodological naturalism, scientism, the limits and scope of scientific knowledge, the intelligibility of nature, and the relationship between scientific inquiry and metaphysical questions.

4. Consciousness & Meaning

Research concerning consciousness, subjective experience, meaning, intelligibility, the relationship between consciousness and reality, and the philosophical implications of contemporary consciousness research.

5. Human Origins & Qur'anic Anthropology

Research concerning Adam, human origins, Qur'anic anthropology, biological humanity, interpretation of Qur'anic accounts of human emergence, and the relationship between Qur'anic anthropology and contemporary evolutionary questions.

6. Divine Justice & Human Agency

Research concerning divine justice, human responsibility, free will, moral agency, accountability, punishment and reward, and the relationship between divine sovereignty and human action.

Research Questions

The recurring questions across this work concern why reality is intelligible; how the Qur'an constructs relationships between signs, meaning, knowledge, and reality; how Qur'anic concepts should be interpreted within their broader semantic and textual contexts; what scientific inquiry can establish about reality; and how human agency, responsibility, and human origins should be understood in relation to divine sovereignty and contemporary knowledge.

Research Approach

  • Qur'an-centered inquiry
  • Internal textual analysis
  • Semantic and conceptual analysis
  • Engagement with classical scholarship
  • Engagement with contemporary scholarship
  • Philosophical analysis
  • Interdisciplinary examination
  • Critical comparison of competing interpretations

This methodological orientation is grounded in the principle of tafsīr al-Qur'ān bi al-Qur'ān, in which Qur'anic passages and concepts are examined in relation to one another, while also engaging classical and contemporary scholarship and attending to philosophical and interdisciplinary questions where they arise. The work is marked by critical examination of competing interpretations, attention to assumptions, and a willingness to distinguish textual evidence from philosophical inference and empirical findings from metaphysical claims.

Taken together, these areas constitute a connected research program concerned with reality, intelligibility, meaning, interpretation, consciousness, human existence, and responsibility. Individual projects and publications provide concrete manifestations of this broader intellectual orientation.
